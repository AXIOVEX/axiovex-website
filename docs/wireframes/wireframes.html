Axiovex redesign — wireframes

Each page of axiovexsystems.com re-structured on a consistent pattern language — sticky nav with CTA, strata bar, premise → services grid → proof → people → CTA band → three-column footer — rendered in Axiovex navy / cyan and the current Axiovex font stack. Dashed boxes are layout blocks; the small cyan labels name each block. Nothing on the live site changes until every wireframe here is approved.

Five adaptations baked into these wireframes — flag anything you want changed

  • 1 · Centered axis kept. Heroes and page heads follow your standing direction — full symmetry: Axiovex keeps its centered titles and one center axis; the pattern language contributes structure, rhythm, and components, never left alignment.
  • 2 · Strata bar = 3 segments. The bar has 3 segments for the 3 services, in cyan-family tints.
  • 3 · No contact form. Our privacy policy states the site has no forms, and there’s no form backend — so the contact wireframe uses a two-column layout whose left column is email routes (start@ / legal@), not a form.
  • 4 · No invented proof. A track-record band would normally carry stats and brand logos; Axiovex has none published, so that band becomes “Evidence in practice” — the latest published analysis plus the AI 101 materials. Real content only.
  • 5 · FAQ stays. The FAQ remains, restyled as clean rows inside the new rhythm.

A full-width alert strip that appears above the sticky nav (WF-G1) on every page — home, blog index, every article, Documents, Signals, Contact, Privacy — when, and only when, a curator has placed a breaking item in data/breaking.json. The whole strip is one link to the source story; a separate dismiss button (not part of the link) hides it for that entry only. The headline is drawn as a placeholder: a wireframe that invented a “breaking” headline would itself be making a news claim.

Nothing renders above the nav — no empty strip, no placeholder space, no script tag. The banner’s absence is total, which is what makes its presence mean something.

  • Curated, never auto-triggered. An entry exists only because a person put it in data/breaking.json and committed it — Tristen, or an agent acting under the monitoring runbook — with the reason it applies to Axiovex’s audience recorded in the file (not displayed). The commit is the audit trail. No keyword trigger promotes a feed item to the banner in v1: a public breaking banner is a claim, and auto-triggers cry wolf.
  • Verbatim, like Signals. The headline is the publisher’s own words, shown with the source name and published time — the same display rule as the Signals lanes (spec 004). Axiovex adds no summary, no opinion, no rewording, and no urgency of its own; “breaking” is the curator’s recorded judgment, not a style.
  • 72-hour maximum life. Every entry carries an expiry at most 72 hours after the story’s published time; the build drops an expired entry on its own. Nothing rides the banner indefinitely.
  • Dismissal is per entry. The ✕ hides the banner and the browser remembers the choice keyed by that entry’s id — so the next, different breaking item shows again. Dismissing is not unsubscribing.
  • Deliberately off-palette. The strip is deep red (#8C2B2B family) with near-white text — the only off-palette element on the site, used nowhere else. An alert has to read as an alert at a glance; brand cyan would make it look like ordinary site furniture and train visitors to ignore it. This is a decision, not drift.
  • Built in, never fetched. The banner is injected by the site generator at build time from the committed data file (spec 004 architecture) — no client-side fetching, no third-party runtime calls. It is first in reading order: a role="region" labeled “Breaking news”, a visible focus state on the link and the button, and no motion of any kind.

Michigan’s class of 2025 graduated at just over 84%, the highest four-year rate since the state adopted the federal formula.
