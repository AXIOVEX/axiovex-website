## 6. Framework

The five working ideas are retained from the current page,
verbatim in substance: (1) start with limits, not stuff;
(2) every view comes through an interface; (3) records are
all we ever get; (4) counting is unforgiving — and that is
its power; (5) the method: register, run, publish — either
way.

**Framework results** (the mechanism statements from the
current page's panel that do not map to a single board
study, stated here as framework results with their open
premises named):
- A counting law for how much information fits inside a
  boundary holds exactly in the model (Study 024 is its
  registered test). The extra condition it needs from
  nature is still open — and we proved the current axioms
  cannot settle it either way.
- Two operations that sound alike — averaging away fine
  detail, and treating symmetric states as identical —
  are provably not the same operation. One of them is
  licensed by the axioms; the other is not.
- For the standard recovery test, the derived failure
  points matched the computations across the whole Petz
  line — and one knob, disorder in the reference, made
  violations rarer as it grew: a noise that repairs
  instead of deepening.

**The five stages** (no attribution): every claim in the
program passes through the same five stages, and a claim
is only ever as settled as its stage.
1. **Created** — the claim is stated, with the test that
   could kill it.
2. **Checked** — the registered test runs and the verdict
   is taken.
3. **Explained** — the reasoning is written out so a named
   reader can follow and challenge it.
4. **Accepted** — independent researchers take the claim
   up and it survives their hands.
5. **Digested** — the claim and its evidence are part of
   how the field works; nothing about it needs us anymore.
Program position, stated on the strip: the claims on this
page stand at **checked** and **explained**; none is
carried as accepted or digested.

**Two lanes.** Checking runs at machine speed — a test can
be re-run in an afternoon. Acceptance moves at human pace —
it takes other people, reading and judging, over months.
The two are tracked separately, and the first is never
displayed as the second.

**Append-only corrections.** A changed assessment is a new,
dated record laid beside the old one. Original records and
issued verdicts are never rewritten.

**The audit (2026-10-12).** The program turned this
discipline on its entire history — every closed study
re-examined, every headline result broken into its parts
and re-scored part by part, with dated digestion records
published for the whole set. Two outcomes are stated on
this page: the consciousness contrast claim (board card 4)
was found to have survived unjudged rather than failed; and
the status counting that used to appear here was retired —
verdicts on this page are stated as what they mean, with
the count of the record shown once, in the ledger above.

## 7. Latest research (collapsible, newest first)

1. **October 13, 2026 — No measurable stand-in for the
   feeling of seeing survives our own rules.** Basis: the
   phenomenal-observable study, program record of
   2026-10-13. Body (from the study document):
   - The question. The program studies what seeing
     *reports* — that side is testable. What seeing *feels
     like* is fenced off, unless something measurable could
     stand in for the feeling without simply being the
     report renamed. This study asked what such a stand-in
     would have to look like for the program to adopt one,
     and scored the serious candidates against the answer.
   - The requirements. Five, each forced by a rule the
     program already runs on: a stated measuring procedure
     anyone can carry out; the quantity must not be report
     renamed; the link between the quantity and experience
     (the bridge) must be checkable against evidence that
     does not come from the quantity itself; the quantity
     must discriminate what experience is *like*, not only
     whether it is present; and it must fit inside the
     framework's scope.
   - What the candidates did. The level of consciousness —
     whether experience is present, and roughly how much —
     is genuinely measurable. The strongest measure
     (perturb the brain, and score how complex the echo
     is) passes the procedure requirement and is plainly
     not report renamed. But it reaches level and presence
     only. And every candidate failed the same requirement
     — the bridge. A bridge can only be checked against an
     anchor, and the only anchor anyone has is report:
     what people say they experienced. Where report
     exists, the stand-in adds nothing the program does
     not already test directly. Where report is absent —
     the unresponsive patient, the no-report trial, the
     non-human system, exactly the cases a stand-in would
     earn its keep — no check is possible, so no kill
     condition exists, and the claim cannot be sorted.
   - The verdict. No candidate survives. One leading
     candidate (a quantity computed from a system's causal
     structure) failed the procedure requirement at its
     own authors' statement. Nothing measurable anywhere
     in the survey touches phenomenal *character* — what
     experience is like, as opposed to whether it is
     there. This is a verdict about what can be adopted as
     a measurement — **not** a claim that experience is
     unreal, or that it is the same thing as a report.
   - What would change it. The study names its exits: a
     complexity measure taken while a patient cannot
     report, registered in advance to predict what the
     patient later reports on recovery, done to standard
     across cohorts; a structural prediction made away
     from the anchor; independent measures converging
     without recovery cases. Until one of those exists,
     the fence stands — by result, not by preference.

3. **October 12, 2026 — The whole history, re-examined.**
   Basis: the framework realignment and full-history
   audit, 2026-10-12. Body (from the audit record):
   - The program adopted a stricter discipline for what
     counts as settled. A claim passes through five
     stages — created, checked, explained, accepted,
     digested — and creation plus checking alone never
     settles it.
   - Every headline result was broken into its parts: is
     the statement faithful? is the evidence verified? is
     the claim genuinely open? is the attribution clean?

     human-paced acceptance. Easily graded counts never
     stand in for understanding, and every verdict states

## 10. Method — how a claim earns its verdict

Steps: 1 · State the claim — and the test that could kill
it. 2 · Register both publicly, before any data is
touched. 3 · Run exactly what was registered — no quiet
adjustments. 4 · Publish the verdict either way — then
explain it, and keep explaining until it is digested.
Standing rules: a falsifier is written before the test,
never after · a rejected claim is published with the same
prominence as a confirmed one · corrections are appended,
never rewritten in · machine-speed checking is never
displayed as human-paced acceptance · what remains not
understood is stated with every verdict.
