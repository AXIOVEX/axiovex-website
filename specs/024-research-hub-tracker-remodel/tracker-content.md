## 6. Framework

The five working ideas are retained from the current page,
verbatim in substance: (1) start with limits, not stuff;
(2) every view comes through an interface; (3) records are
all we ever get; (4) counting is unforgiving — and that is
its power; (5) the method: register, run, publish — either
way. The mechanism statements from the current status panel
that do not map to a board study (the boundary counting
law; the two operations that sound alike; the channel
recovery map) are stated in this section as framework
results, with their open premises named.

**The five stages** (new; no attribution): every claim in
the program passes through the same five stages, and a
claim is only ever as settled as its stage.
1. **Created** — the claim is stated, with the test that
   could kill it.
2. **Checked** — the registered test runs and the verdict
   is taken.
3. **Explained** — the reasoning is written out so a named
   reader can follow and challenge it.
4. **Accepted** — independent researchers take the claim
   up and it survives their hands.
5. **Digested** — the claim and its evidence are part of
   how the field works; nothing about it needs us anymore.
Program position, stated on the strip: the claims on this
page stand at **checked** and **explained**; none is
carried as accepted or digested.

**Two lanes.** Checking runs at machine speed — a test can
be re-run in an afternoon. Acceptance moves at human pace —
it takes other people, reading and judging, over months.
The two are tracked separately, and the first is never
displayed as the second.

**Append-only corrections.** A changed assessment is a new,
dated record laid beside the old one. Original records and
issued verdicts are never rewritten.

**The audit (2026-10-12).** The program turned this
discipline on its entire history — every closed study
re-examined, every headline result broken into its parts
and re-scored part by part, with dated digestion records
published for the whole set. Two outcomes are stated on
this page: the consciousness contrast claim (card 4) was
found to have survived unjudged rather than failed; and
the status counting that used to appear here was retired —
verdicts on this page are stated as what they mean, with
the count of the record shown once, in the ledger above.

## 10. Method — how a claim earns its verdict

Steps: 1 · State the claim — and the test that could kill
it. 2 · Register both publicly, before any data is
touched. 3 · Run exactly what was registered — no quiet
adjustments. 4 · Publish the verdict either way — then
explain it, and keep explaining until it is digested.
Standing rules: a falsifier is written before the test,
never after · a rejected claim is published with the same
prominence as a confirmed one · corrections are appended,
never rewritten in · machine-speed checking is never
displayed as human-paced acceptance · what remains not
understood is stated with every verdict.
