Highlights — the short version

In August 2026, Michigan manufacturing employment rose 1.1k to 586.7k — 12.3k above its January low — while the labor force fell 25.5k to 4.85M and the unemployment rate rose 0.1 pts to 5.0%. Across the twelve months in the snapshot, the labor force is down 151.6k (−3.0%) while total nonfarm employment is up 1.5k (+0.0%). In the published outlooks, nurse practitioners are the fastest-growing occupation on both boards — +41.5% in Michigan’s 2024–34 projections (MCDA) and +41.0% nationally for 2025–35 (U.S. BLS) — and data entry keyers are among the fastest-falling on both (−26.4% in Michigan, −25.5% nationally). Michigan’s class of 2025 graduated at just over 84%, up from 82.8% for the class of 2024.
